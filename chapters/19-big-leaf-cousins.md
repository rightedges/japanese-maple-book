# The Full Moon Cousins: Japonicum and Shirasawanum

While *Acer palmatum* is the undisputed king of the collection, no master garden is complete without its "Big Leaf" siblings. These trees, often referred to as the **Full Moon Maples**, offer a different aesthetic: broader, fanned leaves, more intense neon colors, and a majestic "layered" habit that differs from the sinuous palmatum.

In this chapter, we explore the two primary cousins: *Acer japonicum* and *Acer shirasawanum*, and how their care differs from the techniques we have learned so far.

---

## 1. *Acer japonicum* (The Fern-Leaf / Downy Maple)

The "Downy Maple" gets its name from the soft, velvet-like fuzz on its new shoots. 

### Key Characteristics
*   **The Leaf**: Larger than a palmatum, often with 11 to 13 lobes. The lobes are joined closer to the center, creating a broad, dinner-plate appearance.
*   **The Flower**: *Japonicum* produces some of the most spectacular flowers in the maple world—large, purple-red clusters that hang like jewels in early spring.
*   **Top Cultivars**: 
    *   **['Aconitifolium']({{ '/cultivars/aconitifolium' | relative_url }})** (The Dancing Peacock): Famous for its deeply cut, fern-like leaves and world-class scarlet fall color.
    *   **['Vitifolium']({{ '/cultivars/vitifolium' | relative_url }})**: Large, grape-like leaves with neon orange and gold autumn displays.

---

## 2. *Acer shirasawanum* (The Golden Full Moon Maple)

Often called the "Masterpiece of the Mountain," *shirasawanum* is prized for its exceptional clarity of color and elegant, fan-like foliage.

### Key Characteristics
*   **The Leaf**: Very round (orbicular) with 9 to 13 shallow lobes. The leaf looks like a perfectly opened lady's fan.
*   **Growth Habit**: Tends toward a multi-stemmed, layered look. It grows more slowly than *palmatum* and requires more patience.
*   **Top Cultivars**: 
    *   **['Aureum']({{ '/cultivars/aureum' | relative_url }})**: The definitive Golden Full Moon. Its leaves are a translucent chartreuse-yellow all summer.
    *   **['Autumn Moon']({{ '/cultivars/autumn-moon' | relative_url }})**: Similar to Aureum but with a "burnt orange" blush on the tips in summer.
    *   **['Moonrise']({{ '/cultivars/moonrise' | relative_url }})**: A modern classic; more heat-tolerant than Aureum, featuring neon pink new growth.

---

## 3. The Specialist's Care: How They Differ

The "Big Leaf" cousins are often more demanding than the standard *palmatum*.

### The "Burn" Danger
Because the leaves have a larger surface area, they lose water much faster through transpiration.
*   **Shade is Mandatory**: Species like **'Aureum'** will burn to a crisp in even 2 hours of afternoon sun. They require high canopy shade or a northern exposure.
*   **Wind Sensitivity**: These large leaves act like "sails." In a windy spot, they will tatter and tear at the edges. A protected "pocket" in the garden is essential.

### Soil and Moisture
These species are generally less tolerant of drought. If a *palmatum* wilts, it might recover; if a *shirasawanum* dries out completely, it often suffers permanent tip die-back or death. They require consistently damp (but never soggy) soil and benefit greatly from the **Misting Systems** discussed in Chapter 13.

---

## Summary: A Different Music

Adding a *japonicum* or *shirasawanum* to your collection is like adding a brass section to an orchestra. They provide a structural weight and a "glow" that the finer-textured palmatums cannot match. By respecting their need for deeper shade and higher humidity, you can grow these mountain giants into the crown jewels of your landscape.
