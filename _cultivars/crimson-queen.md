## Overview
'Crimson Queen' is one of the most widely recognized and beloved lace-leaf (dissectum) cultivars in the world. It is the "gold standard" for weeping red maples, prized for its exceptional leaf retention and its ability to maintain a deep, saturated burgundy-red color through the heat of summer better than many other dissectums. It develops a graceful, cascading, and mounding habit that eventually forms a wide, arching canopy of fine foliage.

The tree is moderately vigorous and develops a strong, architectural structure with branches that often reach the ground. It is a long-lived and reliable cultivar that provides a classic Japanese garden aesthetic to any landscape.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: New leaves emerge in a bright, vivid crimson or scarlet-red. The foliage is finely dissected into delicate, narrow lobes, giving the tree a soft, feathery appearance from a distance.
- **Summer**: One of its primary assets is color stability. While many red dissectums fade to bronze or green in high heat, 'Crimson Queen' holds its rich burgundy or dark red color remarkably well, especially when provided with some dappled light.
- **Fall**: A breathtaking transition into a brilliant, translucent fire-engine red. The autumn color is exceptionally bright and provides a high-impact show that is one of the highlights of the garden year.

## Garden Use & Care
'Crimson Queen' is an ideal specimen for a central position in a garden bed, a focal point near a patio, or as a graceful element leaning over a pond or stone feature. Its weeping form and fine texture make it a perfect companion for larger-leaved maples or structural evergreens. It is also a premier choice for large, low containers.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Wind Protection**: The delicate lace-like leaves are sensitive to strong, drying winds which can cause "tip burn." Plant in a sheltered location to preserve the pristine appearance of the foliage.
- **Light Shielding**: Morning sun and afternoon shade is the ideal balance. Direct, harsh afternoon sun in hot climates can cause the fine leaf tips to scorch, even with its superior color retention.
- **Pruning**: To emphasize its arching habit, perform "umbrella pruning" in late winter. This involves removing any branches that are growing straight up or crossing through the center, allowing the cascading branches to take center stage.

> [!TIP]
> Site 'Crimson Queen' on a small mound or near a retaining wall. This historical planting technique allows the cascading branches to drape naturally over the edge, creating a stunning vertical display of feathery red foliage.
