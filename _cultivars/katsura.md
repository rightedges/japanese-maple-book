## Overview
'Katsura' is one of the most famous and widely planted orange-gold cultivars in the world. It is prized for its extraordinary spring display, where the new leaves emerge in a brilliant, shocking arrangement of pale apricot, orange, and soft pink. The name translates to "Wig of the Katsura Tree," referencing the way the small, vibrant leaves seem to layer over one another like a refined headpiece.

The tree has a graceful, bushy, and upright habit that develops into a dense, pillowy specimen. It is exceptionally vigorous and resilient, making it a "workhorse" cultivar that provides high-impact color with very little effort from the gardener.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: One of the most famous sights in the maple world. New leaves emerge in a brilliant tapestry of pale apricot, orange, and chartreuse. The emerging foliage is small and delicate, creating a cloud of warm "sorbet" colors that signal the peak of spring.
- **Summer**: The foliage matures into a healthy, bright mid-green or chartreuse. The tree maintains a dense, reliable canopy that provides excellent shade. Even in high heat, 'Katsura' remains robust and healthy.
- **Fall**: A reliable grand finale. The foliage transitions into a brilliant display of bright yellow, burnt orange, and often a soft, warm red. The fall color is exceptionally vivid and echoes the warm tones of its spring display.

## Garden Use & Care
'Katsura' is an ideal specimen for a mid-sized garden, a focal point in a mixed border, or as a structural element near a patio. Its fast growth rate (up to 12-18 inches/year) and dense habit make it an excellent choice for a tall hedge, a privacy screen, or as a background for smaller, more delicate cultivars. It is also a premier choice for large containers.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Frost Awareness**: Because it buds out quite early, 'Katsura' is susceptible to late spring frosts. Plant in a sheltered location or be prepared to cover it during unexpected cold snaps to protect that spectacular orange spring flush.
- **Sun/Color Relationship**: Needs high-quality light to develop its best orange and gold colors. Morning sun followed by afternoon shade is the ideal balance. While it is sun-tolerant, harsh direct light in hot climates can cause the small summer leaves to lose their chartreuse glow.
- **Pruning**: Responds well to pruning. To maintain its dense, bushy habit, perform occasional thinning in mid-summer or late winter.

> [!TIP]
> Plant 'Katsura' near a dark evergreen or a purple-leaved maple. The contrast between the apricot-orange spring leaves and the darker backdrop is one of the most stunning "spring moments" you can create in a garden.
