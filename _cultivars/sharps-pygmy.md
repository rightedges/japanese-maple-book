## Overview
'Sharp’s Pygmy' is widely considered one of the finest dwarf Japanese maples in existence. Discovered as a chance seedling at Sharp's Nursery in Oregon, it is the quintessential "natural bonsai." It develops a dense, compact, and perfectly globe-like habit with almost no intervention. Because it stays so small (maxing out at 3-5 feet after several decades), it is highly coveted for small spaces, rock gardens, and especially by bonsai enthusiasts who value its small leaf size and dense branching.

The tree presents a level of refined, architectural perfection that is rare even among Japanese maples. It looks like an ancient, gnarly tree in miniature almost from the day it is planted.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: A fresh and lively emergence. Small, 5-lobed leaves burst forth in a bright lime-green or "neon chartreuse." The new tips often have a faint pinkish blush that quickly settles into solid green.
- **Summer**: The foliage settles into a healthy, mid-to-bright green. The leaves are incredibly dense and shingled, providing a "cloud-like" texture to the overall silhouette.
- **Fall**: A breathtaking and reliable finale. The foliage transitions into a brilliant, fiery orange and scarlet-red. The fall show is one of the most intense among the green dwarf cultivars, often looking like a glowing ember in the autumn landscape.
- **Form**: Naturally develops into a compact, rounded globe. It is one of the slowest-growing maples, often only putting on 1-2 inches of growth per year.

## Garden Use & Care
'Sharp’s Pygmy' is a premiere candidate for a garden trough, a rock garden, or a small patio container. It is a "living sculpture" that should be placed where it can be appreciated as a single specimen. In larger landscapes, it is often used at the front of a border to provide a low, structured element.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Sun Tolerance**: Surprisingly sun-tolerant for a dwarf. In northern climates (Zones 5-7), it can handle significant sun. In southern zones (8-9), it appreciates protection from the afternoon sun to prevent leaf margins from "crisping."
- **Minimal Pruning**: This is the ultimate "low maintenance" tree. Pruning is almost never required unless you are training it for bonsai. If you must prune, do so in mid-summer after the leaves have hardened.
- **Moisture**: Requires consistent moisture. Because it is often grown in containers or rock gardens, it is susceptible to drying out quickly. Do not allow the soil to become bone-dry.

> [!TIP]
> think of 'Sharp’s Pygmy' as "instant age." If you want a tree that looks like it has been in the garden for fifty years but you only have a few square feet of space, this is the perfect choice.
