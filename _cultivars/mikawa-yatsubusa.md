## Overview
'Mikawa Yatsubusa' is widely considered the ultimate "collector's" dwarf Japanese maple. It is legendary for its unique, "shingled" foliage where the leaves overlap one another like the scales of a dragon or the tiles on a roof. This creates a dense, multi-layered texture that is absolutely unique in the maple world. It is an extremely slow grower, eventually forming a rugged, sculptural specimen that looks like a natural-born bonsai.

The tree develops a complex, multi-stemmed habit with short, stout branches. Its structural beauty is so intense that it remains a focal point even in the winter dormant season.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: New leaves emerge in a bright, clean chartreuse or lime-green. The "shingling" effect is immediately apparent as the leaves unfold in tight, overlapping clusters. The emerging foliage has a fresh, luminous quality.
- **Summer**: The leaves mature into a solid, healthy mid-green. Because they are so densely packed, the tree creates a solid "mass" of foliage that holds its color flawlessly even in high heat and humidity.
- **Fall**: A spectacular grand finale. The foliage transitions into a brilliant display of golden yellow, burnt orange, and often a saturated scarlet-red. Because the leaves are layered, the fall colors appear in a sophisticated, multi-toned gradient across the canopy.

## Garden Use & Care
'Mikawa Yatsubusa' is the premier choice for rock gardens, small residential entranceways, and as a "living sculpture" in a stone garden. Its extremely slow growth and small size (3-5 feet) make it a perfect candidate for long-term container gardening and high-quality bonsai. It should be planted where its unique texture can be appreciated up close.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Sun Tolerance**: This is one of the most sun-tolerant dwarfs. In most climates (Zones 5-8), it can handle full sun without significant leaf burn, provided it has consistent moisture.
- **Sanitation**: Because the foliage is so dense, it can trap falling leaves from neighboring trees or debris. This can lead to fungal issues if the debris stays wet. Periodically "clean" the branch junctions to keep the interior healthy.
- **Pruning**: Almost no pruning is required to maintain its shape. However, a light thinning of the interior every few years can help reveal the magnificent, rugged branching structure.

> [!TIP]
> Think of 'Mikawa Yatsubusa' as a "sculpture that grows." Plant it near a stone lantern or a decorative basin to create an instant, high-quality Japanese garden vignette.
