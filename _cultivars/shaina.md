## Overview
'Shaina' is a premier dwarf red cultivar originally discovered as a witch's broom on a 'Bloodgood'. It is prized for its extraordinary dense, twiggy, and bushy habit—eventually forming a solid "mass" of foliage that looks like a saturated ruby flame in the garden. It is a slow to moderate grower, eventually forming a well-structured mound reaching just 4-6 feet.

The tree has an exceptionally refined texture, with small, star-shaped leaves that are held in dense clusters. It is noted for its ability to fit into the smallest garden spaces while maintaining a bold, deep-red color profile.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: A spectacular emergence. New leaves burst forth in a brilliant, shocking arrangement of burnt maroon and bright red. The foliage is relatively small and appears in dense clusters, creating a cloud of intense red early in the season.
- **Summer**: Outstanding color stability. 'Shaina' holds its dark burgundy-red color remarkably well through the heat of summer, rarely "bronzing" or turning green even in partial shade.
- **Fall**: A dramatic grand finale. The foliage transitions into a brilliant, translucent fire-engine red or scarlet. The fall display is exceptionally bright and provides a long-lasting show.
- **Form**: Develops a dense, twiggy structure that provides excellent winter interest after the leaves have fallen.

## Garden Use & Care
Due to its small size (4-6 feet) and intense color, 'Shaina' is a premier choice for rock gardens, small residential courtyards, or as a focal point in a mixed border. It is also an exceptional candidate for long-term container gardening and bonsai, where its naturally compact habit and small leaves are highly prized.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Light Requirements**: Performs best in morning sun with afternoon shade. While it holds color well in shade, some direct morning light will intensify its saturated red hues.
- **Soil & Drainage**: Requires well-draining, slightly acidic soil. Like all dwarf cultivars, it is sensitive to "wet feet," so ensure the planting site is naturally well-drained.
- **Minimal Pruning**: Very little pruning is required. Its naturally bushy habit is self-maintaining, but a light thinning of the interior in late winter can help maintain air circulation.

> [!TIP]
> Think of 'Shaina' as a "compact flame." Plant it near a path or a house entrance where its dense texture and brilliant red colors can be appreciated daily.
