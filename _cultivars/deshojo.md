## Overview
'Deshojo' is legendary for having what many consider the most intense and shocking spring color of any Japanese maple. The name translates roughly to "Red-Faced Monk," referencing the fluorescent carmine or lipstick-red that the leaves display upon emergence. This color is so saturated that it practically glows, often appearing "impossible" to those seeing it for the first time.

The tree has a graceful, upright, and somewhat bushy habit. It is perhaps the most famous cultivar for bonsai in the world, but it is equally spectacular as a small specimen tree in the garden. It is a tree that marks the true arrival of spring with a visual bang.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: A horticultural firework. New leaves emerge in a brilliant, translucent, almost neon carmine-red. The foliage is small and appears in dense clusters, creating a cloud of intense color that is the highlight of the early spring garden.
- **Summer**: The intense red slowly transitions through shades of orange and bronze before settling into a sophisticated mid-green. This transition is a beautiful process that often leaves the tree with a multi-toned, "antique" look for several weeks.
- **Fall**: A reliable finale of brilliant scarlet and orange-red. The fall color is exceptionally bright, echoing the intensity of its spring display but with a deeper, more saturated tone.

## Garden Use & Care
'Deshojo' is an ideal specimen for small gardens, courtyard settings, or near a house entrance where its spring color can be appreciated daily. Its moderate size (8-10 feet) and high-impact behavior make it a premier choice for garden focal points. It is also the "holy grail" for many bonsai practitioners due to its small leaves and fine branching.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Frost Protection**: Because it buds out quite early, 'Deshojo' is susceptible to late spring frosts. Plant in a sheltered location or be prepared to cover it during unexpected cold snaps to protect that legendary first flush of red.
- **Sun/Color Balance**: Needs high-quality light to develop its best carmine colors, but the delicate spring leaves can be sensitive to direct heat. Morning sun followed by afternoon shade is the ideal "Goldilocks" zone.
- **Pruning**: Responds well to pruning. To maintain its bushy, dense appearance, perform light thinning in mid-summer or late winter. For bonsai, specialized techniques are used to further reduce leaf size and increase ramification.

> [!TIP]
> Plant 'Deshojo' near a light-colored wall or a stone element. The contrast between the "glowing" carmine-red leaves and the neutral background will make the spring display even more dramatic.
