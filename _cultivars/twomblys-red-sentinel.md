## Overview
'Twombly's Red Sentinel' is a striking architectural Japanese maple discovered by Ken Twombly as a "witch’s broom" mutation on a 'Bloodgood' maple. It inherits the exceptional burgundy color and sun tolerance of its parent but introduces a highly sought-after, narrow, columnar growth habit.

Maturing to a height of 10 to 15 feet but often remaining only 6 to 8 feet wide, it offers a dramatic vertical accent for tight spaces where a standard, broad-canopied maple would not fit. 

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: Leaves emerge a vibrant, bright red in spring, establishing a strong vertical pillar of color. 
- **Summer**: The foliage darkens into a rich, deep burgundy or purple-red, holding its color extremely well through the summer heat, much like 'Bloodgood'.
- **Fall**: A brilliant transformation into a fiery crimson and scarlet display that practically glows in the autumn landscape.
- **Winter**: Even after the leaves drop, the bare stems retain an attractive blood-red coloration, providing valuable structural and color interest throughout the dormant season.

## Garden Use & Care
Due to its slender profile, 'Twombly's Red Sentinel' is an exceptional choice for narrow side yards, entryways, and courtyard gardens. It serves beautifully as a vertical exclamation point in mixed borders and performs very well in large containers.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Light Requirements**: Prefers partial sun to filtered sunlight but tolerates full sun quite well in cooler climates. In extreme heat zones, some afternoon shade will help prevent leaf scorch.
- **Moisture**: Requires consistent moisture, especially while establishing. Do not allow the root zone to dry out completely.
- **Pruning**: Requires minimal pruning to maintain its shape. If any errant branches break the narrow silhouette, they can be pruned in late winter or early summer.

> [!TIP]
> Use 'Twombly's Red Sentinel' to frame an entrance or flank a gateway. Planted in pairs, they create a formal, regal appearance without taking up excessive horizontal space.
