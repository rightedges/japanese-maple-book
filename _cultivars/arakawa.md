## Overview
'Arakawa' is one of the most distinctive cultivars in the maple world, prized not for its leaves, but for its bark. As it matures—usually starting around age 4 or 5—the trunk and branches develop a thick, deeply fissured, "corky" bark that gives it an ancient, rugged appearance far beyond its actual years. This characteristic makes it a legendary choice for bonsai, but it is equally spectacular as a full-sized specimen in the garden.

The tree is exceptionally vigorous and takes on a strong, upright, and somewhat irregular form that heightens its character. It is a tough, durable cultivar that can tolerate more environmental stress than many of its delicate cousins.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: Emerges with a standard but clean green Palmatum leaf. The foliage is healthy and provides a vibrant, green canopy early in the season.
- **Summer**: The leaves mature into a solid, mid-green. The focus during summer is the tree's health and the ongoing thickening of the corky bark on the newer branches.
- **Fall**: The autumn display is a bright, reliable mix of golden yellow and brilliant scarlet-red. The contrast between the fiery fall foliage and the rough, gray-brown bark is one of the most striking sights in the garden.
- **Winter**: After the leaves fall, the "Rough Bark" takes center stage. The corky ridges catch the light and provide an unparalleled architectural texture that persists throughout the dormant season.

## Garden Use & Care
'Arakawa' should be planted where its unique bark can be seen and touched. It is a conversation piece that works perfectly as a focal point in a stone garden or near a patio. Its fast growth rate also makes it a good choice for those who want a larger specimen tree in a relatively short period.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Bark Protection**: The corky bark is the tree's primary asset—avoid physical damage from lawnmowers or string trimmers. Once damaged, the corky ridges can be slow to reform in that specific area.
- **Watering**: While vigorous, 'Arakawa' appreciates consistent moisture. Drought stress can sometimes lead to smaller leaves or earlier-than-expected leaf drop in the fall.
- **Feeding**: This tree has a high appetite for nutrients due to its vigor. An annual application of compost or a balanced organic fertilizer in early spring will support both foliage health and bark development.

> [!TIP]
> The "corking" begins on the older wood first. To encourage bark development on a young tree, focus on health and growth; as the trunk widens, the bark will begin its spectacular transformation.
