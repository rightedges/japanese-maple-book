## Overview
'Bihou', which translates to "Beautiful Mountain," is a relatively newer introduction that has taken the maple world by storm. It is the yellow-barked counterpart to 'Sango Kaku', featuring extraordinary apricot-yellow or golden-orange bark that is one of the brightest sights in the winter garden. Its foliage is a crisp, clean yellow-green that provides a refreshing, high-luminosity presence during the growing season.

The tree has a strong, upright, and somewhat vase-shaped habit. It is vigorous and resilient, though it is slightly more sensitive to certain fungal issues (like *Pseudomonas*) than some other cultivars, requiring a bit more attention to hygiene and siting.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: New leaves emerge in a soft chartreuse or light lemon-yellow, often with subtle pink or orange tips. The emerging foliage is extremely fresh and clean.
- **Summer**: The leaves mature into a solid, healthy yellow-green or mid-green. The tree maintains a dense, airy canopy that provides excellent shade.
- **Fall**: A breathtaking display of brilliant yellow and soft orange. The fall colors complement the golden bark perfectly, creating a "sun-kissed" look that is one of the highlights of the season.
- **Winter**: The bark is the true centerpiece. After the leaves fall, the entire tree—especially the younger wood—glows in a saturated, mouth-watering apricot or golden orange. It is truly unique among maples and provides an unparalleled architectural element in the winter landscape.

## Garden Use & Care
'Bihou' should be used as a focal point where its winter bark can be appreciated. It is often planted as a companion to 'Sango Kaku' or 'Aoyagi' to create a multi-colored bark "symphony." Its upright habit also makes it an excellent choice for a tall specimen in a mixed border or as a structural element near a patio.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Fungal Awareness**: Like many colored-bark maples, 'Bihou' can be susceptible to *Pseudomonas* tip blight, especially in wet, cool springs. Plant it in a spot with excellent air circulation and avoid overhead watering to keep the bark and foliage dry.
- **Encouraging New Wood**: The best bark color is found on the newest growth. To keep the tree "shining" in winter, perform occasional thinning of older branches to stimulate fresh, vibrant golden shoots.
- **Sanitation**: Always use sterile pruning tools to prevent the spread of disease. A light application of copper spray in late winter can also help as a preventative measure.

> [!TIP]
> Plant 'Bihou' where it can be seen from your favorite winter window. Its golden-apricot bark is one of the few things that can truly "turn on the lights" in a grey, overcast winter landscape.
