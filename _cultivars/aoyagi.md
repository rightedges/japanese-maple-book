## Overview
'Aoyagi', which translates to "Green Willow," is the green-barked counterpart to the more famous 'Sango Kaku' (Coral Tower). While it lacks the red winter bark, it offers a stunning, Pea-green or chartreuse wood that persists throughout the winter, providing a refreshing architectural element in a dormant garden. Its foliage is a crisp, clean green that provides a perfect backdrop for more colorful garden companions.

The tree develops a strong, upright vase shape and is notably vigorous, often putting on several inches of growth in a single season. It is a resilient cultivar that handles environmental stress better than many variegated varieties.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: New leaves emerge in a bright, almost luminous chartreuse. The emerging foliage is extremely fresh and clean, typical of the Palmatum group.
- **Summer**: The leaves mature into a solid, healthy mid-green. The tree maintains a dense canopy that provides excellent shade and a cooling visual effect during the height of summer heat.
- **Fall**: A reliable performer. The foliage transitions into a bright, clear yellow, sometimes with subtle orange highlights on the leaf tips. The yellow fall color against the lime-green bark is a standout combination.
- **Winter**: The bark color is most intense after the leaves have fallen. The young wood is a vibrant pea-green, which stands out beautifully against snow or dark winter evergreens.

## Garden Use & Care
'Aoyagi' is an excellent choice for a focal point where winter interest is desired. It is often planted in pairs or groups with 'Sango Kaku' to create a "red and green" winter bark contrast. Because of its fast growth and upright habit, it also works well as a screening tree or part of a tall mixed border.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Bark Vibrancy**: To maintain the best green bark color, encourage new growth through occasional thinning or heading back of older branches. The youngest wood always has the most intense color.
- **Pruning**: Responds well to pruning. It can be kept to a smaller size with regular thinning, though its natural vase shape is its most attractive feature. Prune in late winter or early spring before the fast-rising sap begins.
- **Vigor**: This is a hungry tree; it benefits from a balanced organic fertilizer in early spring to support its fast growth rate.

> [!TIP]
> Pair 'Aoyagi' with 'Sango Kaku' to create a "traffic light" winter interest display—the contrasting red and green barks are one of the best winter sights in the maple world.
