## Overview
'Tiny Stars' is a rare and highly prized dwarf cultivar that is noted for its extraordinarily small, star-shaped leaves and dense, "twiggy" habit. It is an extremely slow grower, but unlike many dwarfs, it develops a rugged, architectural form that looks hundreds of years old even when small. It is a certified conversation piece and the target of almost every Japanese maple collector.

The tree has an exceptionally refined texture, with leaves that are held in dense clusters along the branches. It is a "treasure" for rock gardens and bonsai enthusiasts.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: New leaves emerge in a bright chartreuse or lime-green. The "tiny stars" are immediately apparent as they unfold, creating a cloud of miniature foliage early in the season.
- **Summer**: The foliage matures into a solid, healthy mid-green. The tree maintains its dense, bushy habit, providing a solid "mass" of foliage that holds its color well through the height of summer.
- **Fall**: A reliable finale. The foliage transitions into a beautiful tapestry of golden yellow, burnt orange, and soft red. The fall display is multi-toned and echoing its refined texture.
- **Form**: Develops a dense, twiggy, and naturally rounded mound that provides excellent winter interest after the leaves have fallen.

## Garden Use & Care
Due to its small stature (2-4 feet) and high-quality detail, 'Tiny Stars' is a premier choice for rock gardens, small residential courtyards, or as a focal point in a stone garden. It is also an exceptional candidate for long-term container gardening and bonsai, where its naturally compact habit and tiny leaves are highly prized.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Light Shielding**: Morning sun and afternoon shade is the ideal "Goldilocks" zone. While it handles light well, the best health is preserved by providing protection from the most intense afternoon heat.
- **Soil & Drainage**: Requires perfectly draining, slightly acidic soil. Like all dwarf cultivars, it is sensitive to "wet feet," so ensure the planting site is naturally well-drained.
- **Minimal Pruning**: Very little pruning is required. Its naturally rounded habit is self-maintaining, but a light thinning of the interior in late winter can help maintain air circulation.

> [!TIP]
> Think of 'Tiny Stars' as a "living jewel." Plant it where you can appreciate its tiny, star-shaped leaves up close—perhaps near a seating area or a decorative water basin.
