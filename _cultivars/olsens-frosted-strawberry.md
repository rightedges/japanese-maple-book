## Overview
'Olsen’s Frosted Strawberry' is one of the most celebrated members of the reticulated variegated group, discovered by nurseryman Harry Olsen as a seedling of 'Aka shigitatsu sawa'. It is prized for its high-contrast "frosted" appearance and its compact, graceful form. The tree stays manageable, usually forming a broad, rounded canopy that settles at about 6 feet, making it an ideal choice for urban landscapes, rock gardens, and high-end container culture.

The "frosted" name comes from the way the secondary colors overlay the leaf, giving it a cool, shimmering effect that looks as if it were lightly dusted with sugar even in the peak of summer.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: A spectacular emergence. New leaves burst forth in a radiant strawberry-pink or a "hot rose" color. Each leaf is etched with deep red or burgundy veins that create a visual depth and intricate texture.
- **Summer**: The intense pinks mature into a unique palette of creamy, frosted white and pale, luminous green. The dark burgundy veins remain prominent, providing a sharp contrast against the lighter "field" of the leaf.
- **Fall**: A reliable finale of brilliant fiery orange, scarlet red, and sometimes a deep rust-orange. The fall display is multi-toned and exceptionally bright.
- **Form**: Naturally develops a dense, bushy, and broad-spreading habit. It is an "architectural" dwarf that looks mature even at a small size.

## Garden Use & Care
'Olsen’s Frosted Strawberry' is a premiere specimen for a shaded patio, a residential entry, or as a focal point in a Japanese-style garden. It is particularly effective when planted where it can be viewed from above (e.g., near a deck or a lower terrace) so its intricate leaf patterns are visible. It is also an exceptional candidate for bonsai due to its naturally small leaf size and interesting bark.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Sun Protection**: Critical for success. This cultivar requires protection from the harsh afternoon sun (especially in Zones 7-9) to prevent the delicate white areas from scorching. Morning sun and filtered afternoon light is ideal.
- **Moisture**: Requires consistent, even moisture. It is more sensitive to drought stress than non-variegated cultivars. Avoid "wet feet" through perfect drainage.
- **Pruning**: Minimal pruning is required. Its natural habit is dense and well-structured. Only remove occasional all-green "reversions" if they appear, to maintain the tree's health and genetics.

> [!TIP]
> think of 'Olsen’s Frosted Strawberry' as "cool fire." Its bright pink spring color provides the heat, while its frosted summer appearance provides the cooling relief your garden needs during July and August.
