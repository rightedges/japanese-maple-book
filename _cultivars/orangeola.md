## Overview
'Orangeola' is widely considered one of the finest lace-leaf (dissectum) maples ever introduced. It is prized for its exceptional color transitions and its unusually vigorous growth habit for a weeping tree. While many dissectums are slow to establish, 'Orangeola' quickly forms a dense, elegant mound of cascading foliage that eventually reaches 6-8 feet in height and width.

The cultivar is particularly famous for its "glow"—the way its leaves catch and transmit light, making the tree appear as if it is illuminated from within during its peak orange phases.

## Key Features & Seasonal Interest
- **Spring**: A spectacular emergence. New leaves burst forth in a bright, glossy orange-bronze or "burnt orange." The color is significantly more saturated than the typical reds found in other dissectums.
- **Summer**: The foliage matures into a rich, dark green overlaid with secondary flushes of bright orange on the new growth. This "two-tone" effect gives the tree a dynamic, shimmering appearance throughout the warmest months.
- **Fall**: A breathtaking finale. The entire tree ignites in a fiery, translucent display of bright orange and scarlet-red.
- **Habit**: A cascading, pendulous form that is more upright than 'Crimson Queen' or 'Tamukeyama', eventually forming a multi-layered, architectural dome.

## Garden Use & Care
'Orangeola' is an ideal specimen for a focal point, a rock garden, or as a structural element near a water feature where its weeping branches can reflect off the surface. Its relatively high sun tolerance makes it more versatile than many other lace-leaf varieties.

### Maintenance Tips
- **Light Requirements**: While it handles sun well, it performs best with afternoon shade in very hot climates to prevent the "orange" phase from fading too quickly to green.
- **Pruning**: Minimal pruning is needed. To highlight its layered habit, perform occasional "window pruning" to expose the structural branching within the mound.
- **Soil**: Requires well-drained, slightly acidic soil. Ensure the root flare is slightly above ground level to prevent root rot.

> [!TIP]
> Plant 'Orangeola' in an elevated position, such as on a mound or near a retaining wall. This allows the cascading branches to flow downward, maximizing the visual impact of its weeping architecture.
